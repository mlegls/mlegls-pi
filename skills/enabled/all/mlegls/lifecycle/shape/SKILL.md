Carry this issue to ready: enough consensus on what to do that the rest happens autonomously. Ready names the motivating situation and desired outcome, settles the human choices, has a check that observes the outcome, and waits on no unresolved human or external blocker (`tracker` lifecycle). How it splits into sessions is not part of readiness. Each step adds to its body (`tracker`). Grill consequential choices and resolve discoverable facts through research or experiments; own exploratory workers and interpretation of their results. Stop at ready or at a specific external input that cannot be obtained here.

1. Destination, where it isn't agreed: use `grill` to agree on it, what is out of scope, and what is already decided. Record decisions so far and holes, each with how to resolve it; a hole is its own issue when it's a session or shared. Fill the holes worth filling first.
2. Locate the motivating story: the real situation, want and originating request or observation. Extend an existing story where it fits; refactors cite the stories they preserve.
3. Before settling implementation shape, find which responsibilities existing machinery can discharge. Use `grill` where the maintainer contributes intent, theory or UX judgment. Resolve implementation choices within the agreed promises autonomously; decision difficulty is not a human dependency. If implementation constraints threaten a promise, explain why at the program-theory surface before asking which promise to revise. Sketch the program with `show-me` where useful. Stories hold intended outcomes; the issue holds implementation shape and unresolved holes.
4. Make first use possible: identify the starting state, user surface and setup needed to try the change. Include missing fixtures or drive machinery in the assignments. Plan the experience, not an assertion inventory. Tests in an issue: see `testing`.
5. Prototype anything users will see with me while shaping, in this session or a top-level sibling of the grilling thread (`artboard`), and link the approved screens from the issue: a drawing child leaves workers drawing just in time and queueing it for my review. One that changes what users see isn't ready until I've approved its prototype. Retain useful artifacts as references after their results are incorporated.
6. Split where this session holds context that would make better children than the reconciler's fresh decomposition later; otherwise mark work larger than a session `decompose: true`. Write children independently executable, with their dependencies. Cut each as a tracer through its most different consumer; to de-scope a consumer, thin it rather than move it out, since an interface proven only on similar consumers hard-codes their similarity. Research and technical prototype children can be ready while the enclosing goal remains unsettled; review them through interactive feedback.
7. Update the durable stories and issues (`project-docs`, `tracker`). The guide is written just before or during first use, alongside its recording; implementation assignments include that handoff.

A subtree goes to supervision whole: bring every child to ready, or move it out of the tree, before handing it over.

The tracker is the handoff: don't message a supervisor that work is ready; a project's pickup (`supervise`) takes newly ready trees in its scope. Hold `claimed-by` on the tree while you shape it and release it when the tree is ready, so it isn't taken half-written. Tell the supervisor named in `desk/ROLES.md` only what needs it: a changed contract of a tree it is already running, or a decision it must make; with none, add it through `ab desk attention add` (`attention`). Return what remains before `implement` or `supervise`.
