---
name: research
description: "Use when a question needs investigation against primary sources."
---

Follow a claim to the source that owns it. Local code establishes local
behavior; the library, platform, paper, or original artifact establishes its
own claims. Time-varying facts need current evidence.

Investigate the uncertainty that matters to the decision. Preserve citations
and contradictions without forcing them into a coherent story. Distinguish
what the source says from what you infer.

Confirm the scope before substantial investigation or fanout, and the proposed
account before publishing it into shared context (`grilling`). Quick lookups
can help frame that proposal.

Delegate independent reading when there is useful work to do alongside it.
A short answer can stay in the conversation; research another session needs
belongs in a linked note or on the tracked question. The deliverable is the
answer with its evidence and remaining uncertainty, not a compulsory report.
